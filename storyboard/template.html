Story

Agains

The story is still there: the girl in the white room, the man sent to find her, the three things he offers her, the run, the cutting, the years. But it's told the way a memory feels from inside, not shown as events.

She is never drawn. The film is her memory: real photographs, faded and soft, surface on the beat and drift through the haze at different depths, the way memories come and go. Early on they're daylight and home: a shore, a curtain, rain on a window. When he arrives they turn to night: a figure in a doorway, a car window, streetlights in fog. He is never drawn either; he's warmth leaking in from outside the frame, and as she gets lost inside him it colours the memories. His three virtues are three shafts of that light, with his words lit inside them; in the cutting each word is excised and sewn shut in red thread, and what they leave him (GUN, BULLET) stands where his virtues were, and the bullet falls between them. The white coats aren't people. They're the overexposure itself, the fluorescent flicker, the scratches and their cold typed voice.

Time is literal in only one place: a tally mark scratched slowly into the wall on every bar, the same faint presence in every scene, 146 by the end, the last landing on the last downbeat. It goes only while they run; on 'But...' the uncounted bars scratch back in. It's also in the image itself. An exposure echo keeps earlier moments ghosted into the present, so the film gets more layered as it goes; after the cutting she can't hear, and every repeated line is written over how it looked the first time. In a film where everything is soft, the falling bullet in 'A gun and a bullet' is the only thing ever in sharp focus. The title is never shown plainly until the very end: through the film 'lost and' is only an unreadable echo where 'found...' lands; at the end it surfaces, faint, with nothing after it.

This grew from your Mage: The Ascension premise, with the mood taken from the track art. No character is depicted in detail, the institute has no emblem, and nothing is drawn from the game.

Look

Long exposure of a memory

Bleached lavender haze, lifted milky blacks, fog that moves, dust in the air. The optics do most of the work: bloom, film halation (warm fringes bleeding off bright edges), a diffusion filter, lens fringing at the edges and grain. Warm light is the only saturated colour, and it always means him.

This matches the track's split: breathy, reverb-drenched vocals over cold, clinical textures, with a sing-song chant cutting through.

Typography

Three voices, three typefaces

Her · sung lead
She got lost inside him

EB Garamond Italic: her telling, leaning the way she is lost. A word of hers stands upright in roman only when it can't be taken back (the choice: no, yes; the outcome: lost, found; the fate: once, never, forever, ever, again; the turn: But...), one per line, and the title is always roman. Italic never appears outside her voice. Each word focus-pulls out of blur on its sung onset and sheds two ghost exposures that drift apart and linger. It's dark ink in the haze and white-hot with halation in the dark.

White coats · chant
Scribble scribble, stab stab the charts

IBM Plex Mono caps, typed on letter by letter and perfectly still. A "stab" slams in whole and is slashed underneath in pencil; what they mean to cut out of him is marked round with an incision and sewn shut in red thread, the same surgery his virtues get later. When she sings of them coming round the bend, even her words are in their type.

Him · spoken
I won't lose you

Inter Light caps with wide tracking, rendered as warm light through a crack. The words never move, like his dry close-mic voice.

After you approve

  1. Build scene by scene, one module per section, all driven by the timing files.
  2. For each scene, render a contact sheet (one frame per second, stamped with timecode and bar) and run automatic checks: every word visible within one frame of its onset, text inside title-safe, no text colliding. Fix and re-sheet until clean.
  3. Speed up the renderer first. It takes about 1.2 s a frame now. Then render 1920×1080 at 30 fps in parallel chunks, each with a warm-up so the exposure echo is continuous across the joins, as H.264 CRF 17 with the original audio as AAC 320k.
  4. Verify with ffprobe: stream specs, frame count against duration, audio and video lengths within one frame, and sync spot-checks at three lyric onsets.
